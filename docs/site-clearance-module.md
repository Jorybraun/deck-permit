# Site-clearance module

`site-clearance/v2` derives reproducible written proposed-work-to-parcel reference dimensions from the same local project coordinate system used by the editor and permit drawings.

The shared proposed-site-component graph contains every platform polygon, stair-flight corridor, explicit grade-landing rectangle, and generated footing pad. For every municipal parcel segment, the engine finds the shortest segment-to-segment distance across all of those polygons. Intersections and boundary contact produce a zero distance. Every component vertex is also tested against the reference parcel polygon. The result records the governing component kind and identifier, closest points, parcel-edge identifier, written millimetre distance, and whether the whole modeled proposal appears contained by the municipal reference.

The authority is always `municipal-gis-reference`. A1.0 and the calculation report display this provenance next to the dimensions and state that GIS geometry is not a legal survey. The readiness evaluator fails closed until a dimensioned survey or field-measurement PDF is attached to the exact design fingerprint. Owner evidence remains `recorded-unverified`; the application does not upgrade it to survey evidence or claim municipal acceptance.

This separation lets homeowners position a design intelligently while preserving the professional and municipal evidence boundary needed for a permit package.

`site-compliance/v2` adds the planning screen. The homeowner explicitly selects the parcel edge along the street as the front property line, identifies the rear property line, and may identify an exterior street-side line for a corner lot. The remaining edges are treated as interior sides. It then compares the governing platform, stair, landing, or footing clearance at every parcel line with the corresponding recorded zoning setback. Compass direction and geometric distance are never used to invent front or rear yards.

The same module calculates reference lot coverage from an exact polygon union of all municipal building reference polygons plus modeled platforms, stair flights, and grade landings, so stacked or overlapping regions are counted once. Footing pads participate in setback screening but are not added to lot coverage. The plan reports existing, proposed-platform, proposed-stair/landing, proposed-union, combined, lot, and maximum coverage values. This remains a screening calculation: municipal rooflines may include eaves, and the authority must decide which deck, stair, landing, and projection areas count under the bylaw.
